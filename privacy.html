Privacy Policy

Last updated September 6, 2026

Your routine, profile and saved products are personal. This page explains what stays on your iPhone and what leaves it.

Face analysis — with your permission

Before your first face analysis, we ask permission to send the photo through Supabase to Anthropic. We resize the image and remove embedded metadata. Anthropic returns estimates of visible cosmetic features; the app does not identify you or diagnose a medical condition. We do not store uploaded photos or results in our server database.

Anthropic's standard commercial API policy deletes inputs and outputs within 30 days, with exceptions such as safety enforcement and legal requirements. API content is not used for model training by default. We do not promise immediate deletion by every processor. See Anthropic's commercial retention policy.

You can withdraw AI photo permission in Settings. This blocks future face-analysis requests until you agree again; it does not erase past provider records. Your saved history remains accessible.

What stays on your iPhone

Your optional name, goals, skin type, sensitivity, routine preferences, ingredient exclusions, saved products, routines, progress, skin check-in notes (including comfort, dryness, irritation and product changes) and saved face photos are stored in the app's private storage. Product labels are read using Apple's on-device Vision text recognition. Product matching and routine composition run on your phone; your profile and product-label photos are not sent to Anthropic.

The app does not provide automatic cloud sync. Backup & transfer lets you create a password-encrypted file containing your profile, scans, photos, shelf, check-ins and routine progress, and import it on another iPhone. We cannot recover its password. You choose where to save or share the file, including any file-storage provider. Subscription entitlements, device identifiers, scan allowances and analytics preferences are not included. App data may also be included in iPhone backups or device transfers according to your Apple settings. Deleting local app data does not remove exported files or existing backups.

Usage analytics and subscriptions

In production builds, PostHog receives selected interaction events such as onboarding completion, paywall views and subscription actions, plus app/device information and an anonymous installation identifier. PostHog receives the network address and can derive an approximate location (such as city or country) from it; we do not request GPS or precise location access. We do not send skin scores, findings, profile answers, product ingredients or photos to PostHog. Session replay and automatic screen capture are disabled. Turn off usage analytics in Settings to stop future app analytics events. The consent-filtered subscription-event service, when enabled, checks current consent in RevenueCat before sending lifecycle events and an analytics identifier to PostHog. The service processes subscription metadata through Supabase without retaining webhook payloads in an app database. Consent changes reach that service when the app can synchronize with RevenueCat; changes made offline take effect there after synchronization.

RevenueCat manages subscription status using its app-user identifier and Apple's transaction information. With usage analytics enabled, we associate the PostHog identifier with RevenueCat to measure subscription conversion. These are pseudonymous identifiers, not a claim that events cannot be linked to the same installation. Apple processes payments; we never receive your payment card details.

Acquisition measurement

We report coarse onboarding, trial and purchase milestones through Apple's SKAdNetwork framework. We do not request an advertising identifier or send skin data to ad platforms. Campaign codes in links may be stored locally and included in permitted usage analytics. Apple's attribution reporting and subscription records are separate from the optional PostHog analytics setting.

Abuse prevention and operating the service

Scan-availability checks and face-analysis requests carry a random installation identifier stored in the device keychain. Supabase receives the network address as part of processing requests. We store salted hashes of the installation and network identifiers with request counts to enforce limits, and remove those counter rows after 35 days during service maintenance. Keychain identifiers may survive reinstalling the app; deleting local history does not reset service allowances.

Operational logs record request status, processing time and model token counts, not photos or skin findings. These technical diagnostics help us operate the service and monitor performance. Hosting providers may retain ordinary infrastructure logs under their service settings. These records and purchase records are not automatically erased when the app is removed. We do not sell personal data.

Catalog and retailer links

The app fetches a verified product catalog from GitHub Pages. Opening a manufacturer or Amazon link contacts that website, which applies its own privacy policy. Amazon links include our Associates tag. We receive commission for qualifying purchases, not your skin data.

Your choices and deletion

Use Progress to record, edit or delete local check-ins, or delete an individual scan and its photo. Settings lets you delete your profile, scans and history, turn off analytics, withdraw AI permission and manage reminders. Local notifications are scheduled on the phone with your permission. Deleting data does not cancel your Apple subscription; use Manage Subscription.

For a request about provider-held records, contact us. Because we do not require a name or account, we may need the relevant app-user identifier or transaction details to locate those records. Some purchase, fraud-prevention or legally required records may need to be retained.

Children

Glowscope is not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect their information.
